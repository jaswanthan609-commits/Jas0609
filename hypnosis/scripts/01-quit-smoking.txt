# Session 1: Quit smoking. Listen once a day from about a week before your quit day, and every day for the first month after.
# Techniques: self-affirmation of values (Epton 2015), your own reasons for quitting (motivational interviewing), future-self imagery,
# urge surfing (Bowen and Marlatt 2009), if-then plans (Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006), a calm-breath anchor.
# No disgust or fear suggestions: "modern" permissive hypnosis did better than classical forms (Flammer and Alladin 2007).
@title Breathe Free
@subtitle A self-hypnosis session for quitting smoking
@include _induction

From this calm place, think of something that matters deeply to you.
Not about smoking. Something you value.
Perhaps your family. Your health. Your freedom. Your work. The people who count on you.
[pause 8]
Let one of these come forward now.
Notice why it matters to you.
Maybe there's a moment, a face, or a feeling that goes with it.
[pause 10]
This is who you are.
A person with things worth protecting, and the strength to protect them.
[pause 6]

Now, from that same place, let your own reasons for being smoke-free come to mind.
Your reasons. Not anyone else's.
[pause 4]
Maybe it's breathing easily when you climb the stairs.
Maybe it's the money you'll keep.
Maybe it's tasting your food again, or waking up without that heavy chest.
Maybe it's simply wanting to be free of something that has been deciding things for you.
[pause 10]
Let the most important reason grow clear and bright.
Hold it for a moment.
[pause 8]

Now imagine yourself a few months from today.
You have been smoke-free all this time.
[pause 4]
See where you are. See what you're wearing.
Notice how you are standing. A little taller, perhaps. Lighter.
[pause 6]
Take a deep breath as that future you.
Feel how clean and open that breath is.
The air goes all the way down, and comes back out easily.
[pause 8]
Notice what you can smell. What you can taste.
Notice the quiet pride of keeping a promise to yourself.
[pause 8]
That future you is not someone else.
It is you, a little further along the same road.
Every smoke-free hour is a step toward that person.
[pause 6]

Now let's prepare for the moments that used to bring a cigarette.
Urges will come. That's normal. It isn't failure, and it isn't a sign that you need to smoke.
An urge is like a wave.
It rises, it peaks, and then, if you don't feed it, it falls away. Usually within a few minutes.
[pause 6]
Imagine an urge arriving now. Just a small one.
Notice where you feel it. The chest, the throat, the hands.
[pause 5]
Instead of fighting it, get curious.
Breathe into it, slowly.
Watch it the way you would watch a wave out at sea.
[pause 8]
Rising.
[pause 4]
Peaking.
[pause 4]
And beginning to fall.
[pause 6]
You rode it. You didn't have to do anything else.
You are the surfer, not the wave.
[pause 6]

Now we'll set your calm-breath anchor.
Gently press your thumb and first finger together.
[pause 3]
As you press, take one slow breath in.
And let a long breath out, longer than the breath in.
[pause 6]
Feel the calm that comes with it.
From now on, whenever you press your thumb and finger together and breathe out slowly, this calm returns.
It gets stronger every time you use it.
[pause 4]
Release your fingers.
[pause 4]

Now picture the moments that used to mean a cigarette.
Perhaps the morning tea.
Perhaps after a meal.
Perhaps when work gets stressful, or when friends step outside to smoke.
[pause 5]
For each one, you have a plan.
If I finish my tea and feel the pull, then I press my thumb and finger, breathe out slowly, and stand up to do something else.
[pause 5]
If stress rises at work, then I take three slow breaths and drink a glass of water.
[pause 5]
If friends offer me one, then I say, no thanks, I've quit, and I stay right where I am.
[pause 5]
See yourself doing each of these, easily.
The plan runs on its own, like a well-worn path.
[pause 8]

And if you are using nicotine gum, lozenges, or patches, or medicine your doctor gave you, see yourself using them exactly as directed.
They are tools on your side, working with you.
[pause 6]

You might notice, over the coming days, that the pull becomes weaker.
That the gaps between urges grow longer.
That you think about cigarettes less and less.
[pause 5]
And when you do think of them, it can be with a calm, settled feeling.
That chapter is closing.
[pause 6]
You are someone who breathes freely.
[pause 4]
You protect what matters to you.
[pause 4]
And you can ride any wave.
[pause 8]
