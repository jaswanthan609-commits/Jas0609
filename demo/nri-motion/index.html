NRE or NRO? If you live
abroad, picking the wrong account can
cost you real money in tax.
Here are the three differences that
matter.
One. Where the money comes from.
NRE is for money you earn
abroad and send into India. NRO
is for money you earn in
India: rent, dividends, or a pension.
Two. Tax in India. Interest on
an NRE account is tax free
in India. On an NRO account
it's taxable, and the bank deducts
thirty percent, plus four percent cess,
before you even see it. A
tax treaty can lower that, if
you file the right forms.
Three. Taking money back out. NRE
money, with its interest, goes back
abroad freely. From NRO, the limit
is one million US dollars per
financial year, after tax, with forms
fifteen C A and fifteen C
B.
One catch. Tax free in India
doesn't mean tax free everywhere. Live
in the US? You still report
NRE interest on your US tax
return.
